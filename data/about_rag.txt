**Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)** is an AI technique that combines information retrieval with Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate accurate, relevant, and context-aware responses. Unlike traditional LLMs that rely primarily on knowledge learned during training, RAG enables models to retrieve information from external knowledge sources, such as PDFs, documents, databases, websites, and internal application data, before generating an answer. The process typically involves two main stages: **retrieval and generation**. First, documents are collected, cleaned, and split into smaller chunks. These chunks are converted into numerical representations called **embeddings** using an embedding model and stored in a vector database. When a user submits a query, it is also converted into an embedding, and a similarity search is performed to retrieve the most relevant document chunks. These retrieved chunks are then combined with the original user query and passed as context to an LLM. The LLM uses this additional context to generate a meaningful response grounded in the retrieved information. RAG helps reduce hallucinations, improves factual accuracy, and allows applications to work with domain-specific or frequently updated information without retraining the underlying model. It is widely used in **AI chatbots, enterprise knowledge assistants, customer support systems, document analysis, and intelligent search applications**. Popular tools and frameworks used to build RAG pipelines include LangChain, LlamaIndex, FAISS, ChromaDB, Pinecone, and OpenAI embedding models.
